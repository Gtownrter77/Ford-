# American-Made Tool Sponsorship Shortlist

**Prepared for Patricia**  
**Date:** August 17, 2026

## Bottom line

For a **published, immediately usable cash affiliate program**, **Warwood Tool** is the strongest verified option I found: it states a **10% commission**, a **30-day cookie**, affiliate support, and customized marketing materials. Its catch is that its American-forged tools center on railroad, forestry, construction, and industrial work rather than vehicle repair. [1]

For the **best published free-tool / discount / loaner perks**, **Jonard Tools** is the clear winner: its ambassador program lists free tools or merchandise, **50% off qualifying tools**, tool-kit loans, rewards points, and additional “Jonard Cash.” It requires two product posts plus one story or live session per month. Jonard is especially relevant to electrical, HVAC, cable, and telecom work, not general automotive hand tools. [2] [3]

For the **best premium automotive-tool name to approach for a custom sponsor deal**, the right first call is still **Snap-on**. It belongs at the top of the app’s premium professional-tool presentation because the brand has direct garage credibility. I did **not** find a public Snap-on creator/affiliate rate or ambassador-perk schedule, so this would be a custom sponsorship conversation—not an off-the-shelf affiliate enrollment. Product origin must be checked by individual SKU; the company is a global manufacturer, so the app should not label the whole brand “American Made.” [4]

## Recommendation: run two partnership lanes

The best real-world approach is not picking one company for every purpose. Use **two lanes**.

**Lane one: immediate affiliate revenue.** Apply to **Warwood Tool** because its public terms are specific and its payout is higher than the 5% Würth USA program. Put it in a clearly marked “Preferred U.S.-Forged Work Tools” sponsor card. This starts the partnership system with a public program, not a long sales cycle.

**Lane two: premium automotive sponsorship.** Pitch **Snap-on first**, then **Channellock**. This app is not an ordinary tool-review page—it is a practice-first repair mentor for a specific 2004 Ford Explorer Sport Trac. The pitch should offer a premium, clearly sponsored “Professional Tools for This Job” placement, not a promise that every tool recommendation will be determined by payment. If Snap-on declines or has no current program, Channellock gives the strongest verified product-level U.S.-made story for everyday pliers, drivers, pry bars, and wrenches. [5]

Jonard is a **very good third outreach** because it can support the A/C, electrical, and wire-repair content with actual loaner/tool and ambassador benefits. Its clear public perk schedule makes it worth testing, but the company should be marketed as a trade-specialty partner, not a broad automotive-tool sponsor. [2] [3]

## How affiliates should appear inside the app

Reputable affiliates are appropriate for this project. The better design is **visible preference, not hidden preference**.

| App area | Recommended treatment | What the customer sees |
|---|---|---|
| **Default catalog** | Premium / professional brands first, including Snap-on tools where product availability and fitment are recorded. | “Premium Choices” is the default drop-down selection. |
| **Sponsor card** | A partner can occupy a top-of-page sponsor spotlight. | A plain label such as **“Sponsored by Channellock”**, **“Snap-on Preferred Professional Tool Partner”**, or **“Affiliate partner”** remains on the card. |
| **Job lesson** | Partner tools can be highlighted as the preferred professional kit for the repair. | The card says whether the app may earn from a purchase and links only after the customer chooses to visit the retailer. |
| **Comparison control** | The customer can switch to Lowest Delivered Total, Local Pickup, Best Warranty, American Made, or other choices. | The selector stays available and the sponsor is not disguised as a neutral price result. |
| **Origin label** | “American Made” is reserved for verified, item-level claims. | “Verified Made in USA,” “Assembled in USA,” “U.S. content claimed—verify,” or “Origin not disclosed” depending on the record. |

This approach gives a sponsor a real commercial reason to participate: the brand gets premium visibility, a preferred-brand association, links, and dedicated repair-tool placements. At the same time, the app is honest about the relationship, and the customer can change the view when a different priority matters.

> A sponsor does **not** have to be invisible to be fair. The proper rule is that the sponsorship should be **plainly identified**, and any fitment, safety, or origin statement must still be true.

The FTC states that a material connection includes money, free or discounted products, and other valuable perks; the connection should be disclosed clearly with the endorsement. [7] An unqualified “Made in USA” claim is appropriate only when the item is “all or virtually all” made in the United States, and mixed-origin product lines need individual treatment. [8]

## Outreach order

1. **Snap-on** — request a custom premium automotive sponsorship / training-tool partnership. Ask for demo tools, a promotional budget, affiliate tracking if available, and permission to use approved brand assets within clearly marked sponsor placements.
2. **Channellock** — propose a verified U.S.-made garage-basics sponsor area, focused on its specific domestic product categories. Ask for a direct affiliate code or quarterly sponsorship arrangement.
3. **Warwood Tool** — apply through its public affiliate program for an early revenue test, limiting promotion to relevant heavy-duty work-tool lessons.
4. **Jonard Tools** — apply to the ambassador program for A/C, electrical, wiring, and connector-tool content, subject to the program’s stated content commitments.
5. **Würth USA** — optional automotive service-shop affiliate partner if the goal expands beyond strictly American-made tools.

## A short sponsorship pitch

> We are building a practice-first repair companion for the 2004 Ford Explorer Sport Trac. Before a user touches the truck, they can rehearse the job on a to-scale model, hear safety guidance, identify the required tools, and compare parts. We would like to feature **[Brand]** as a clearly labeled Preferred Professional Tool Partner for relevant repairs. The app will show the brand prominently in the default Premium Choices view, include approved product links, and identify the sponsorship plainly. We are seeking a partnership that may include affiliate tracking, product support for demonstrations, approved creative assets, and a launch sponsorship.
