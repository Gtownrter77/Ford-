# 2004 Ford Explorer Sport Trac: Custom Single-to-Dual Tailpipe Guide

## Scope and source status

This guide uses the supplied factory-service-manual archive as the primary source. The extracted bundle contains 9,188 HTML pages and is titled **2004 Ford Explorer Sport Trac 2WD V6-4.0L VIN K Flex Fuel**. The target vehicle is 4WD, so the factory procedures and torque values below must be cross-checked against the 4WD VIN K source before treating dimensions, routing, or replacement-part fitment as final.

The manual does **not** describe a factory conversion from one rear tailpipe to two. It documents a factory exhaust with a dual-converter Y-pipe, dual three-way catalytic converters, and a muffler/resonator assembly leading to the rear tailpipe. The procedure below is therefore a **custom dual-tailpipe conversion**, not a factory repair procedure.

## Recommended architecture

For the least invasive and most serviceable conversion, retain the factory exhaust manifolds, dual-converter Y-pipe, oxygen sensors, catalytic converters, and front connection. Replace only the single-outlet muffler/resonator assembly with a correctly sized **single-inlet, dual-outlet muffler**, then route two separate tailpipes through the existing rear-exhaust envelope. This produces dual rear exits without adding unnecessary catalytic converters or changing the emissions-controlled portion of the system.

Do not install an unverified second catalytic converter, remove or relocate oxygen sensors, or split the exhaust upstream of the catalytic converters. Those changes are not supported by the supplied manual and can create emissions, calibration, heat, and inspection problems.

## Factory torque values applicable to retained components

The exhaust torque plate on manual pages 2275 and 5261 gives the following values:

| Connection | Torque |
|---|---:|
| Muffler-to-converter bolts and nuts | **40 N·m (30 lb-ft)** |
| Converter-to-manifold nuts | **40 N·m (30 lb-ft)** |
| Dual converter Y-pipe to dual three-way converter nuts | **40 N·m (30 lb-ft)** |

Do not apply the 40 N·m factory value to custom muffler clamps, custom hanger bolts, or aftermarket hardware unless the hardware manufacturer specifies it. Torque those components to the selected manufacturer’s instructions or the fabricator’s documented hardware specification.

## Step-by-step procedure

### 1. Confirm the conversion will not disturb emissions hardware

Before buying parts, identify the two converter outlets, both oxygen sensors, the Y-pipe, the existing muffler/resonator assembly, and the single rear tailpipe. The manual’s exhaust description on page 2269 identifies the Y-pipe and dual three-way catalytic-converter arrangement. The conversion boundary should be **after the factory catalytic-converter outlet**.

Measure the existing inlet pipe outside diameter, flange pattern, muffler body envelope, and available routes on both sides of the vehicle. Record the position of the fuel tank, spare tire, frame rails, brake lines, wiring, rear shock, stabilizer link, axle housing, bed floor, and bumper. Repeat this measurement on the actual 4WD truck; do not assume the 2WD archive geometry is identical.

### 2. Choose the muffler and route on paper first

Select a single-inlet, dual-outlet muffler that physically fits in the factory muffler/resonator area. Lay out two tailpipes that leave the outlets without crossing the driveshaft, axle, fuel tank, shock, or stabilizer hardware. The two pipes should be independently supported and should have enough movement allowance that the axle cannot strike either pipe at full jounce or rebound.

Keep both exits behind the passenger compartment and away from openings into the cab or bed. The tips should end in a position that cannot direct exhaust toward the rear hatch, side windows, or any body seam.

### 3. Prepare the vehicle safely

Place the transmission in NEUTRAL and support the vehicle on a properly rated hoist or stands at approved support points. The factory muffler/tailpipe procedure on pages 8747 and 8750 begins with the vehicle in NEUTRAL on a hoist. Allow the exhaust to cool completely. Disconnect the battery if welding near sensitive wiring, and protect fuel lines, wiring, and plastic components from sparks and heat.

### 4. Remove the factory rear assembly

Following pages 8747 and 8750, remove the muffler-to-converter flag nuts, bolts, and springs, and discard the old torque-prevailing hardware. Remove the shock absorber bolt only as required to move the shock out of the way, and position the RH stabilizer-bar link out of the way as described by the manual. Detach the tailpipe from its rubber isolator, then detach the muffler from its isolators and remove the muffler/tailpipe assembly.

Do not lubricate the rubber isolators with oil or grease. The manual warns that these substances deteriorate the rubber and can allow the hanger insulator to separate from its bracket during vehicle operation.

### 5. Inspect and prepare the retained flange

Clean the converter outlet and the replacement inlet mating surfaces. Remove all gasket debris without gouging the flange. Install a new converter-outlet gasket. Replace the exhaust fasteners as required by pages 8747, 8750, 8319, and 2269.

Inspect the shock mount, stabilizer link, rubber isolators, converter flange, and nearby heat shields while the rear assembly is removed. A damaged or loose component in this area may also explain a passenger-side click during body roll.

### 6. Mock up the dual-outlet system before welding

Install the new muffler loosely at the factory inlet location. Temporarily position both tailpipes and hanger brackets. With the suspension at normal ride height, verify clearance to the frame, bed, fuel tank, spare tire, axle housing, shock absorber, stabilizer link, brake lines, wiring, and bumper.

Cycle or safely simulate suspension movement before final welding. Maintain clearance at full jounce and rebound, not merely at ride height. Leave room for thermal expansion and drivetrain movement. The factory alignment procedure on page 2274 directs the technician to loosen the exhaust, align it from the front to establish maximum clearance, and then tighten from the front toward the rear.

### 7. Install the retained factory flange

Fit the new gasket and loosely install the new muffler-to-converter hardware. Do not fully tighten yet. Keep the muffler supported independently so its weight does not hang from the converter flange or a single rubber isolator.

Once the joint is seated and aligned, tighten the muffler-to-converter bolts and nuts to **40 N·m (30 lb-ft)**. This is the factory value from pages 2275 and 5261. If the actual 4WD source gives a different value, use the 4WD value instead.

### 8. Fabricate and install the two tailpipes

Fabricate the left and right pipes from the dual muffler outlets. Keep each pipe as smooth and direct as practical. Avoid sharp reductions, low-hanging loops, and routing that places either pipe against the frame or fuel tank. Tack-weld or loosely clamp the pipes while the vehicle is supported, then recheck clearance with the suspension loaded and unloaded.

Install separate hanger rods and fresh rubber isolators so neither tailpipe is supported only by the muffler outlet. The second pipe must have its own support. Ensure the rubber is loaded vertically or in the intended hanger direction rather than twisted sideways.

### 9. Final alignment and tightening

Use the factory alignment principle from page 2274: begin at the front, align the retained factory exhaust connection for maximum clearance, then work toward the rear and tighten all factory fasteners and custom clamps or welds. Confirm that the muffler is not rotated into the bed floor and that both pipes have equal movement allowance.

Do not use the factory 40 N·m value for unlisted custom clamps or hanger hardware. Use the clamp or hardware manufacturer’s torque specification. If a shop welds the system, require the shop to document the tubing size, material, weld quality, hanger locations, and final clearance inspection.

### 10. Leak and interference test

Start the engine and inspect every joint for leaks, as required by page 2274. Do not use bare hands near hot exhaust; use approved smoke or leak-detection methods. Listen for contact while the exhaust is cold and again as it reaches operating temperature. Recheck after the first heat cycle because the system will move as it expands.

Confirm that neither pipe contacts the shock, stabilizer link, axle, frame, bed, fuel tank, spare tire, brake lines, wiring, or bumper. Confirm that exhaust gases cannot enter the cab or bed. If the vehicle develops a click, rattle, or body contact after the conversion, stop and correct the clearance rather than simply tightening the hanger.

## What the archive does and does not establish

The archive establishes the factory component arrangement, factory muffler service sequence, replacement-gasket and new-fastener requirements, exhaust alignment method, and the three listed 40 N·m torque values. It does not establish a factory dual-rear-tailpipe conversion, a verified dual-outlet aftermarket part number, or 4WD-specific custom tailpipe dimensions.

For the target 4WD vehicle, the final part selection should be based on measurements from the truck and cross-checking with the 4WD VIN K exhaust pages. The safest budget approach is usually to retain the factory Y-pipe and converters, use one correctly sized single-in/dual-out muffler, and have the two tailpipes fabricated and clearance-checked by an exhaust shop rather than buying a generic “dual exhaust” kit that may interfere with the 4WD underbody layout.
